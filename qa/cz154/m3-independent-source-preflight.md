### **Defect F1 — Source's *reason* is not taught in final source explanation (M3 philosophical-comprehension acceptance gap)**

**Severity:** P1 for the M3 learning hypothesis; **not** an authorization to polish frozen M2.  
**Source:** Long §1 paragraphs immediately following the opening distinction: misattributing external possessions/responses to personal authority exposes one to obstruction, disturbance and others' judgments. The §1 argument is more than two buckets of things.  
**Traces:** From the actual archive, `request → delivered_no_reply → invite` compact “Back to the Philosopher” reads: “Epictetus places our judgment and chosen acts within our power, not others' assent. You sent an invitation; dialogue and public repair remain undecided. An invitation does not create a conversation.” The full section adds an invitation-specific qualification and optional-conversation example but **still no reason why misplacing one's value/agency in the reply is a mistake**. `explain → unqualified` compact/full similarly state the post is completed and reception unknown without the source's argumentative reason. The same opening clause is recycled across **18/18 full and 18/18 compact** source sections, none explaining how making one's judgment contingent on an external response exposes one to being governed or disturbed by it. The source's why is not stated in alternate synonyms about external dependency in the source section. The final questions invite reflection but do not fill the argument gap.

**Why it matters:** A first reader may memorize “I can choose, not control a reply” yet be unable to explain **why Epictetus draws that distinction** or apply it independently in a new case; CZ-153 expressly says the argument must be intelligible at reconsideration and transferable. The real user-learning magnitude cannot be measured without participants; this is a **documented text-level omission**, not a fabricated participant outcome.  
**Narrow corrective request, only after independent gate decision:** In the existing source-authored expression language (not hardcoded Epictetus runtime branches), incorporate a one-sentence warrant explaining *why* dependence on an external response makes the quality of one's judgment hostage to events or other people's choices. Preserve the 5 refinement classes, all 18 statuses, no false causality, and **both** compact and full essential qualifications; keep within 40–65/80–120 or fail closed. Re-evaluate actual 18 text outputs for the new argument, philosopher-swaps, word-count gates and uncoached comprehension. No UX redesign or new event.

### **Defect F2 — Outstanding responsibility is easily missed on unanswered-request pathways (editorial qualification)**

**Severity:** P2; targeted reader check required before any implementation.  
**Source/contract:** Long §1 does **not** directly prescribe apology etiquette; this is expressly the scenario/editorial qualification in CZ-153: non-control of another's response is not license to neglect one's conduct or public accountability.  
**Trace:** `request → correct` has `acknowledged=false` and only a privately prepared future acknowledgment. Compact reads “You offered a conversation without first naming the interruption. You privately prepared a public acknowledgment,” followed by “Preparation is an act, not its future performance.” **It never explicitly says the original acknowledgment remains to be made.** `request → prepare_question` also has `acknowledged=false`, yet its compact conclusion remains “Preparation is an act, not its future performance.” `request → invite` has `acknowledged=false`, but the output discusses the optional invitation without clearly saying that an initial acknowledgment still remains possible or outstanding. Other statuses are consistent: this is an **insufficiently explicit teaching qualification**, not a false assertion that apology already occurred.
