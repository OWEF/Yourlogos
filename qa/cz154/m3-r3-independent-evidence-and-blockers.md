This makes the fictional *other person's reply* a possible application; it is not a statement about apology etiquette or a guarantee of forgiveness.

**Stoic technical term:** Epictetus assigns *one's own assent to impressions* to the rational faculty; see https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/ and https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/. A different person's assent/approval is outside one's control.

**Reproducible F-R3-01, P2 editorial/source ambiguity:** All **18/18 compact** “Back to the Philosopher” source sections say `Epictetus: Your acts are yours; assent isn't. In **18/18 full** sections, the source writes `Your acts are yours; another's assent isn't`, restoring the necessary qualification. Without “another's,” the compact line can deny one's *own* Stoic assent, contrary to the source. This is directly observable copy ambiguity; it is **not** proof that participants were confused or that an external editor has rejected it.
