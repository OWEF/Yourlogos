## Scope of the source readings

Dillard’s passage moves between individual hours and the schedule that can sustain work. The pilot treats repetition as hypothetical. The sliders do not establish what came first or whether rest served a purpose. [Scheduling passage](https://www.saltproject.org/progressive-christian-blog/2023/4/24/how-we-spend-our-days-by-annie-dillard)

Kierkegaard’s catalogue quotation is a shortened formulation of JJ:167 (1843). The fuller entry brings out why a complete retrospective standpoint is unavailable while we live. The job, the events and all possible responses are invented examples. [Danish text and translation note](https://homepage.divms.uiowa.edu/~jorgen/kierkegaardquotesource.html)

## Founder review gate

The joint writing-and-rest path remains the least revealing when the repetition paragraph is skimmed. Kierkegaard’s imagined alternative can also draw attention away from the timing of knowledge. Ask a first-time reader to explain the quotation in their own words and identify the moment in their path that made it clearer.
