## Exact witness and claim

- **Inherited ID 47** is George Long's English opening of *The Encheiridion* §1, “Of things some are in our power, and others are not.” Project Gutenberg eBook 10661 identifies George Long and reproduces this sentence at *THE ENCHEIRIDION, OR MANUAL*, I: <https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm>. The passage distinguishes opinions, impulses, desires and acts from such things as reputation and offices, which depend on others. Its later instruction is to examine an appearance by asking whether it concerns what is ours.

**Claim permitted by the cited §1:** a person can select judgments and responsible actions but cannot command another person's answer, forgiveness, reputation or belief. A reply can be influenced by conduct without being guaranteed by it. Treating the friend's reply as proof of one's own virtue misconstrues this division.

**Analogy limit:** Epictetus does not prescribe how to apologize, how long to wait, how many messages to send, or when interpersonal repair is complete. The fictional first message was delivered and no reply arrived later; that does not establish reading, motive, anger, acceptance, forgiveness, or a causal effect of wording. A preparation in private does not establish performed conduct. These are editorially documented scenario facts and qualifications, not direct words of Epictetus.
