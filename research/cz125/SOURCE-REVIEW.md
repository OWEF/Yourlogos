### 2. Søren Kierkegaard, Journal JJ:167 (1843)
Observed fixture: research:kierkegaard:future-v1; explicitly shortened English aphorism and contextual review pending.
Strong scholarly lead: Kierkegaard Research Centre, University of Copenhagen, confirms JJ:167, SKS vol. 18 and Danish passage:
- https://teol.ku.dk/skc/om-soeren-kierkegaard/soeren-kierkegaard-citater/
- https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/

**Substantive interpretive risk:** The familiar backward/forward aphorism compresses the journal entry. The longer passage continues to say that life within time is never fully intelligible because one cannot attain a settled backward viewpoint. An ending that implied hindsight grants *complete* knowledge would be unsound. The current fixture correctly says the unchosen job future cannot be observed; editorial review must check that the entire three-stage explanation preserves the stronger temporal limit. Real job choices and simulated external events must remain clearly hypothetical; the second stage is **authored development, not reader action**.
Human reviewer must independently select/approve a named English translation or provide properly attributed translation, exact 1843 passage, and confirm each explanation remains source-faithful. **Source location supported; precise translated quotation and substantive interpretation still require independent signoff.**

- https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Walden_(1854)_Thoreau/Economy

### 4. Adam Smith, Wealth of Nations, Book I, Chapter I (1776)

- https://www7.adamsmithworks.org/documents/division-of-labor-and-the-wealth-of-nations

The fictional booklets-workshop comparison may demonstrate **positive, zero or negative modeled productivity under its own invented rates**; a zero-gain case cannot be presented as empirical confirmation of Smith's broader thesis. Independent review should verify that output explicitly distinguishes general source argument from individual no-gain case. Edition/section signoff pending.
